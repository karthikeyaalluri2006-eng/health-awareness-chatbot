Dengue Awareness Guide

Dengue is a viral infection spread mainly by infected Aedes mosquitoes. Dengue can range from mild illness to severe disease, and symptoms may overlap with other infections.

Common symptoms of dengue include sudden fever, headache, pain behind the eyes, muscle and joint pain, nausea, vomiting, and a skin rash. Symptoms commonly begin several days after infection, but timing can vary.

Warning signs requiring urgent medical evaluation include severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding from the nose or gums, blood in vomit or stool, extreme tiredness or restlessness, rapid breathing, pale or cold skin, or difficulty staying awake. Warning signs can appear when the fever starts to improve.

People with possible dengue should seek medical advice for testing and monitoring, especially children, older adults, pregnant people, and people with chronic health conditions. Do not self-diagnose based on symptoms alone.

Prevention includes avoiding mosquito bites by using insect repellent, wearing clothing that covers the skin, using window screens or bed nets when needed, and removing standing water where mosquitoes can breed.

Treatment decisions should be made with a healthcare professional. Ask a clinician or pharmacist which medicines are appropriate, because some medicines can increase bleeding risk when dengue is suspected. Seek emergency care for severe symptoms or rapid deterioration.

Source: World Health Organization (WHO), Dengue and severe dengue fact sheet; Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), About Dengue.
