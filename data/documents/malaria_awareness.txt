Malaria Awareness Guide

Malaria is a serious disease caused by Plasmodium parasites and transmitted mainly through the bites of infected female Anopheles mosquitoes. Malaria is not spread by ordinary casual contact between people.

Common symptoms include fever, chills, sweating, headache, muscle aches, tiredness, nausea, and sometimes vomiting or diarrhea. Symptoms can resemble other illnesses, so testing by a qualified healthcare professional is needed for confirmation.

Malaria transmission can occur wherever infected mosquitoes are present. Risk is often higher during and after rainy seasons because standing water can increase mosquito breeding, but the timing varies by location, climate, and local mosquito activity.

Seek urgent medical care for suspected malaria with confusion, seizures, difficulty breathing, severe weakness, jaundice, repeated vomiting, or worsening illness. Malaria can become severe quickly, especially in young children, pregnant people, older adults, and people with reduced immunity.

Prevention includes using insect repellent, wearing long sleeves and trousers, sleeping under insecticide-treated bed nets, using screens, and reducing standing water around homes. People travelling to malaria-risk areas should ask a qualified travel-health professional about location-specific prevention.

Do not self-diagnose or self-treat malaria. A healthcare professional should guide testing and treatment.

Source: World Health Organization (WHO), Malaria fact sheet; Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), About Malaria.
