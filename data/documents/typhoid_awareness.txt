Typhoid Awareness Guide

Typhoid fever is an infection caused by the bacterium Salmonella Typhi. It most often spreads when food or water is contaminated and then consumed. Risk depends on local sanitation, water safety, and travel conditions.

Common symptoms can include prolonged fever, headache, weakness, stomach pain, loss of appetite, nausea, and changes in bowel habits. Symptoms can overlap with other illnesses, so testing by a qualified healthcare professional is needed for confirmation.

Seek prompt medical care for persistent fever or worsening illness. Urgent care is important for confusion, severe abdominal pain, repeated vomiting, dehydration, bleeding, or difficulty staying awake.

Prevention includes drinking safe water, eating safely prepared food, washing hands with soap, improving sanitation, and following travel-health advice. Typhoid vaccination may be recommended for some people based on travel or local risk; ask a qualified healthcare professional about the appropriate vaccine and schedule.

Do not self-diagnose or self-treat typhoid. A healthcare professional should guide testing and treatment.

Source: World Health Organization (WHO), Typhoid fact sheet; Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC), Typhoid Fever.
